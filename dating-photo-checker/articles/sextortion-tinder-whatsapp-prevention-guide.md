# Sextortion on Tinder and WhatsApp: A Complete Prevention Guide

Dating apps like Tinder, Bumble, or Badoo have revolutionized the way we meet new people. Unfortunately, they have also become the preferred hunting ground for international cybercrime networks specializing in **Sextortion (Sexual Blackmail)**.

This type of scam has seen an alarming increase in recent years. Victims are often emotionally manipulated, only to be later threatened with the destruction of their social reputation, career, and family relationships.

In this guide, we explain step-by-step how this scheme works, what the red flags are, and what concrete measures you can take to protect yourself.

---

## 🔍 What is Sextortion and how does it work?

Sextortion is a form of blackmail in which an attacker convinces you to send intimate photos or videos (nudes or semi-nudes), and then threatens to send them to your family, friends, co-workers, or post them on social media if you do not pay them a sum of money (usually via crypto transfers, Revolut, or gift cards).

Here is the classic scenario in which blackmailers operate:

### Step 1: The Catfish Profile
The attacker creates an extremely attractive profile on Tinder or Bumble. They use photos stolen from foreign influencers, Instagram models, or, more recently, **faces completely generated by Artificial Intelligence (AI)** that cannot be found through simple Google searches.

### Step 2: Quickly moving to other apps
After just a few messages on Tinder, your match will ask for your phone number or Instagram/Snapchat account under various pretexts (*"I don't spend much time on this app"*, *"the chat here keeps freezing"*). Their real goal is to find out your Instagram or Facebook account to extract your friend and follower list.

### Step 3: Rapid escalation to intimate discussions
The conversation very quickly becomes sexual in nature. The attacker will send you intimate photos (which are, of course, stolen from someone else) to make you feel comfortable and will pressure you into sending similar images.

### Step 4: The Trap and Blackmail
As soon as you send a compromising image or video clip, the friendly behavior instantly vanishes. The attacker will send you a screenshot of your friend list from Instagram or Facebook, along with your intimate photo, telling you: *"If you don't send me €500 in 10 minutes, I will send this photo to your entire contact list."*

## 🚩 Red Flags to Watch Out For

If you notice any of these behaviors in a match on a dating app, stop the conversation immediately:

*   **The profile seems "too perfect":** It only has professional, studio, or luxury vacation photos, without any casual photos or simple selfies.
*   **Wants to move off Tinder immediately:** Asks for your Instagram or WhatsApp within the first 3 messages.
*   **Refuses live video calls:** Always makes excuses like a broken camera, poor connection, or being shy, even though they pressure you to send photos.
*   **The language used is strange:** Often uses automated translations or shows unnatural language patterns.
*   **Asks for intimate images very quickly:** The pace of the conversation is artificially rushed.

## 🚨 What to do if you are already being blackmailed?

If you have fallen into this trap, panic is your greatest enemy. Attackers count on your shame and fear. Follow these strict steps:

1.  **NEVER PAY!**
    If you send them money, you will not solve the problem. On the contrary, you prove to them that you are a profitable victim and they will demand even more money. The blackmail does not stop after the first payment.
2.  **Block them on all channels:**
    Stop responding to any messages, threats, or calls. Block the number on WhatsApp, the Instagram account, and the Tinder account. Most of the time, if the attacker sees that they no longer have any leverage over you and cannot get money, they will give up and move on to the next victim.
3.  **Do not delete the evidence:**
    Take screenshots of all conversations, their profile, phone number, and the bank/crypto account they sent you for payment. You will need them for the police.
4.  **Report to the Police (Cybercrime Division):**
    Sextortion is a serious crime (blackmail and illegal access to computer data). File an official criminal complaint.
