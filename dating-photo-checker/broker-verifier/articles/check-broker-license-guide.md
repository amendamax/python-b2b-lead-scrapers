# How to Verify a Forex Broker's Regulation & License (Step-by-Step)

Choosing a Forex broker is one of the most critical decisions you will make as a trader. While low spreads and high leverage are attractive, the single most important factor is the **safety of your funds**. 

Regulated brokers are legally required to protect your deposits, keep client funds segregated, and follow strict anti-fraud standards. Unregulated brokers can freeze your account, manipulate charts, and vanish with your money.

Fortunately, you do not have to take a broker's word about their license. You can verify it yourself. Here is your step-by-step guide to verifying any Forex broker's regulation and license.

---

## 🔎 Step 1: Find the Licensing Information on the Broker's Website
Legitimate, regulated brokers are proud of their licenses and display them prominently.
*   **Where to Look:** Scroll to the very bottom of the broker's homepage. You will usually find a legal disclosure paragraph.
*   **What to Look For:** Look for the name of the operating company (e.g. *XM Global Limited* or *Ava Trade EU Ltd*), the regulator's name, and the license number.
    *   *Example:* "Ava Trade EU Ltd is regulated by the Central Bank of Ireland (No. C53877)."

> ⚠️ **Warning:** Scam brokers often write generic text like *"We are a regulated global entity"* without naming a specific regulator or license number, or they list fake registration numbers from offshore business registries (which are not financial regulatory licenses).

---

## 🔎 Step 2: Cross-Reference the License on the Regulator's Official Register
Scam brokers frequently lie about their licensing or clone the license numbers of real, legitimate brokers (a practice called **clone firm scams**).
To verify the license:
1.  Go to the official website of the regulator the broker claims to be licensed by:
    *   **UK (FCA):** [register.fca.org.uk](https://register.fca.org.uk/)
    *   **Europe (CySEC):** [cysec.gov.cy](https://www.cysec.gov.cy/)
    *   **Australia (ASIC):** [connectonline.asic.gov.au](https://connectonline.asic.gov.au/)
    *   **USA (NFA):** [www.nfa.futures.org/basicnet/](https://www.nfa.futures.org/basicnet/)
2.  Enter the license number or the company name in the search bar.
3.  **Verify the Details:**
    *   **Status:** Ensure the status is listed as "Authorised" or "Active" (not suspended, lapsed, or canceled).
    *   **Approved Domains:** Reputable registers like the FCA list the official website URLs associated with the license. Ensure the domain you are trading on matches the one listed on the official register. If the register lists `broker.com` but you are using `broker-fx24.com`, you are trading on a clone site!

---

## 🔎 Step 3: Check the Domain Registration Age (WHOIS Lookup)
Most scam brokers are short-lived. They set up a site, run it for 6-12 months, steal deposits, get flagged by regulators, shut down, and relaunch under a new name.
*   **The Check:** Perform a WHOIS lookup on the broker's domain name to check when the website was created.
*   **The Red Flag:** If a broker claims on their homepage to be a "trusted global leader since 2015," but their domain registration shows the website was created 3 months ago, they are lying.
